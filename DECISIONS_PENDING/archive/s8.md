**D26 — composed body drawn INSIDE its parent's disc: may the new pick pre-empt old picking?** `[= ledger A37; §11.106(e), A17, §11.52(b)]`
Composed / new-only bodies are now selectable by name and by click (§11.106). The click route runs **only where the old picker declines**, which is what keeps old-body selection unchanged by construction. The old picker does NOT decline inside a drawn body's disc: `searchAround` returns the parent as its `default_object` and `cleverFind` keeps it whenever the best candidate is a body — so a click on a rover drawn on the Moon selects the **Moon** [measured on both binaries: a 150 km composed body at NDC radius 0.38, inside the Moon's 0.699 disc ⇒ `'Moon'`; the assert is locked in `b24_select.py` leg P6, so a later change that flips it fails loudly]. That case is exactly the composition mandate's own scene (*"rover on a moon"*, §11.78(a)), and A17's resolved criterion — *"if a body can be seen, we must be able to select it"* — says it ought to be selectable. Letting the new route win there **changes which body an old-body click selects**, which is the one thing the parity baseline does not let an executor decide.
Question: inside a parent's disc, does a visible composed child take the click (A17 applied literally, at the cost of a changed old-body outcome), or does the parent keep it until the old picking path retires?
Derived from source and deliberately NOT run: the same composed body IS clickable from a **surface** observer, where `searchAround` skips the home body's disc test — so the gap is regime-shaped, not absolute.
`[Fable rec]` (added by the supervising session, 2026-07-25): **let the visible child take the click, when and only when the click lands ON the child** (its drawn disc covers the pick, or its centre is within the pick tolerance) — i.e. extend the §11.106(d) two-tier rule across the old/new seam. If you prefer zero old-click changes until the old picker retires, that is option (2) — safe but regime-inconsistent meanwhile; the P6 assert locks whichever you choose and fails loudly on any later flip.
