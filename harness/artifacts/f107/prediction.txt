2. P2 -- WHY ERIS AND NOT THE OTHER ELEVEN, AS A MECHANISM
----------------------------------------------------------
The prediction is NOT "Eris is the unlucky one"; it is that the selection is
STRUCTURAL and predictable from the branch alone:

  (a) `ell<0.2` (Arrokoth, Ceres, Charon, Hydra_, Nix) is the FIXED-POINT
      branch `lastE = M + e*sin(lastE)` (orbit.cpp:524-525).  Its error after
      ONE step is `|e*(sin a - sin b)| <= 2e` for ANY seed, however stale --
      the map is a contraction with a bounded first step -- so a 75-radian-stale
      seed is gone in one call and the five calls finish at 1e-09 or below.
  (b) `cometEll` (Haumea, Juno, Makemake, Pallas, Sedna, Vesta) WRAPS its mean
      anomaly into [0, 2pi) (`fmod`, iterative_orbits.hpp:125-127), so its seed
      staleness is bounded by one revolution BY CONSTRUCTION and no unwrapped
      75-radian offset can exist on that branch.
  (c) `ell0.2-0.9` -- Eris's Newton branch (orbit.cpp:532-533) -- is the only
      one that sees the UNWRAPPED offset, because `positionAtTime` computes
      `meanAnomalyAtEpoch + (JD-epoch)*n` without any wrap (orbit.cpp:581), and
      **Eris is its only PARKED member** (the other 11 bodies of that branch are
      all walked satellites).
  Consequence predicted: **exactly one of the twelve is slow, and it is the one
  whose branch cannot wrap and whose place denies it a second use.**
  FALSE IF: any `ell<0.2` or `cometEll` parked body shows a res5 above 1e-06 on
  the pre arm (which would refute the boundedness argument), or if Eris's
  branch-mates were parked too.

3. P2b -- WHAT THE MUTATION WILL MOVE, BY NAME AND BY VALUE
-----------------------------------------------------------
The mutation (the constructor's evaluation skipped when `parent->lastJD == 0`)
changes EVERY body's initial seed, so the prediction must cover every body it
touches.  A WALKED body is re-evaluated every frame and has thousands of
evaluations by the P0 dump, so it is converged in both arms; only the PARKED
iterating records (evalCount 5 at P0) can still carry the difference.

  PREDICTED: **exactly TWO of the 120 records move, Eris and Sedna**, and they
  move in OPPOSITE directions (`model_replay.txt`, the float32 identity table):
      Eris  control [85.8409882, 38.323143, -18.7250576]
            mutant  [85.1671219, 39.6249275, -17.3216801]   (= the CONVERGED
            value, i.e. the mutant FIXES Eris: res5 0, P0 sep in the 1e-05 deg
            float floor class, `evalCount` still 5)
      Sedna control [39.3564529, 70.9922104, -17.0377331]    (converged)
            mutant  [-35.3826714, 69.4490356, -7.60469437]   (75.35 AU off:
            res5 0.273577 rad)
  MECHANISM of the Sedna half, stated so the direction is not a coincidence:
  `IterativeEll`'s state starts at (H, c, s) = (0, 1, 0) and its first step from
  that state is `H = M/(1-e)` -- for e = 0.8588315 a jump to 44 radians -- so
  for a comet-family body the constructor's JD-0 call is what BURNS that bad
  first step; removing it hands the bad step to the first real use instead.
  **This is why the mutation is a measurement and not a fix**: one line moves
  one body onto its answer and another off it.
  The other ten parked iterating records are predicted IDENTICAL on all three
  arms at float32, and the 40 walked iterating records identical as well (named
  risk: a 1-ulp difference in a walked body's converged double would print the
  same float32, so this arm cannot see one -- if a walked record DOES move, that
  is the finding).
  FALSE IF: a third record moves, or either named record does not.

4. P3 -- THE SAMPLER CANNOT REACH A PARKED BODY, AND `planets_orbits` CANNOT
   REACH AN ITERATING ONE AT ALL
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Two gates, both structural:
  (a) `propagateRenderHidden` -> `ModularSystem::unregisterBody` ERASES a parked
      body from `sortedSystemBodies` (ModularSystem.hpp:260-267), and
      `ModularSystem::drawOrbits` -- the ONLY caller of `OrbitModule::update`
      for a far body -- walks exactly that list and additionally breaks at
      `distance == 0` (ModularSystem.cpp:630-655).  So no parked body's ORBIT
      module is ever updated and `sampleOrbit` never runs for Eris.
  (b) the field's `flag_planets_orbits = false` (config.ini:280), so the flag is
      OFF at launch even for the bodies the walk does reach.
  NEW PRECISION, and it corrects the task section's own wording: `wantShown`
  routes `body->isSatellite() ? showSatellites : showPlanets`
  (OrbitModule.cpp:91) and `isSatellite()` is "the parent is neither primary nor
  a system" (ModularBody.hpp:1128-1130).  **Every one of F104's 40
  walked-iterative records is a satellite**, and every Sun-parented iterating
  body is PARKED -- so `flag planets_orbits on` samples only closed-form
  (*_special/custom) orbits and reaches NO iterating solver seed.  The flag that
  reaches one is `flag satellites_orbits on`.  Predicted counts: under
  `planets_orbits` **0** iterating bodies sampled; under `satellites_orbits`
  **31** (the 40 walked iterating records minus the **9** whose section carries
  no `orbit_visualization_period` -- Despina, Galatea, Helene, Janus, Naiad,

  FALSE IF: the walk reaches a parked body, or a non-satellite iterating body

6. P5 -- THE IN-ENGINE LEG: A NEGATIVE PREDICTION WITH ITS OWN BLIND SPOT NAMED
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
On the DELIVERED binary e411b838, pinned clock (`timerate rate 0`, `date jday
2461233.5`), one launch with `flag satellites_orbits on` (and one with
`flag planets_orbits on`) against one with both off, dumps at F100's cadence:

  P5   PREDICTED BIT-IDENTICAL on all 120 records, `mat` translation and
       `altaz_new` alike.  REASON: at a pinned clock `sampleOrbit` runs ONCE
       (the `|date - lastSampleJD| >= inc` test never fires again at a frozen
       date), the walked body is re-evaluated EVERY frame at 2 steps per call,
       and the dump arrives hundreds of frames later -- so the transient has
       decayed to the fixed point long before the dump reads it.
       BLIND SPOT, NAMED IN ADVANCE: the transient lives for ~1 frame (7 ms at
       the config's 144 fps cap) and the TCP command channel cannot place a dump
       inside a named frame, so THIS CHANNEL CANNOT SEE IT even where it
       happens.  A null here is therefore NOT evidence that the transient is
       absent -- P4's slice measurement is the evidence, and this leg's own
       positive content is (i) the reach/flag structure of P3 and (ii) the
       existence check below.
  P5b  A second arm sends `flag satellites_orbits on` IMMEDIATELY before the
       dump (no settle).  If the dump lands in the frame right after the first
       sample, the walked iterating records WILL differ, by up to P4's
       magnitudes.  Either outcome is informative and both are pre-registered:
       a difference REFUTES P5's "bit-identical" as a general statement and
       measures the transient in the engine; no difference is the timing blind
       spot above.
  P5c  EXISTENCE CHECK (so that a null is not read as "the sampler did not
       run"): one screenshot per arm, non-photometric -- the count of pixels
       that differ between the flag-on and flag-off frames must be NON-ZERO
       (orbit lines drawn).  Predicted: non-zero for both flags.
  CONTROL that shows the comparison ABLE to fail: the same dump at two
  DIFFERENT pinned dates (2461233.5 and 2461234.5) must differ on many records.
  Predicted: differs on >= 100 of the 120.
