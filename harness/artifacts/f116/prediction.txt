0.  THE ONE OPEN CHOICE THE SECTION LEFT, AND WHAT I PICK
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
The section states the choice as: at the bound, rotate (a) the channel being
written, or (b) all five channels.  I pick NEITHER verbatim: I pick

    (c) ROTATE THE CHANNEL THAT HOLDS THE MOST BYTES (live + its archives).

Why not (a), the argument being the reason this task exists at all.  The budget
is a TOTAL over five channels, so the channel that is over it and the channel
being written need not be the same one.  Take the state this very task is built
for -- a session that has already written ~1 GiB into script.log -- and let the
scripting stop while a quiet channel keeps writing (tcp: a connect line; the
internal channel: a warning per minute).  Under (a) EVERY such line finds the
total over the budget and rotates the TINY channel, which frees its 13-byte
oldest archive, i.e. nothing; after eight such lines that channel's whole window
is empty and each further line rotates it for zero bytes freed while appending
one ~450-byte D12 line to the internal channel.  That is a rotation per line
forever and an internal log that grows without bound: the very defect Sec.5.115
records, recreated by its own fix.  Under (c) the big channel is the one that
rotates, so the excess is always the thing being shed, and the total falls by
construction within at most LOG_RETENTION_LAUNCHES rotations.
Why not (b): a chatty channel would consume a retention slot on four channels
that wrote nothing, so one busy session erases the launch history of every other
channel for no space gained.  (c) consumes exactly one slot, on the channel that
grew.
In the field case the three rules agree: the channel being written IS the
largest (script under the tester's shows, Sec.11.218(m)).  (c) differs from (a)
only in the pathological state above -- and that difference is MEASURED here,
not argued: prediction 8.
Both (c)'s own consequences are named as veto points in the entry: an in-session
rotation consumes one of the eight launch slots, and at the bound a channel
whose single live file is comparable to the whole budget loses its history
within a few rotations (prediction 5).

     (the counter lives IN the channel record that holds the ofstream, so no

     cost is BELOW 200 ns per line and below 2 % of what one cLog::write
     already costs, because every line already does an std::endl flush plus an

5.  WHAT THE BOUND COSTS IN HISTORY (the honest consequence, stated up front)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
5.1  An in-session rotation consumes one of the eight launch slots: after it the
     channel reaches fewer LAUNCHES back.  The owner's "1 GiB TOTAL" accepts
     this by construction; it is a veto point in the entry.
5.2  When ONE live file is comparable to the whole budget, the window collapses:
     the burst of 4.3 deletes the big file within LOG_RETENTION_LAUNCHES
     rotations, so after a crossing the channel keeps only the fresh file.  In
     the FIELD (1 GiB, script at 193 MB/h, Sec.11.218(m)) I derive: the first
     crossing comes after ~5.5 h, frees only the oldest previous-launch archive
     (~10^5 B), so the next crossing follows ~2-10 s later, and after 7 such the
     1 GiB file itself leaves -- i.e. a ~40 s grace, then the session's own
     history is gone and it starts again from ~0.  Bounded and correct against
     the owner's sentence, but it is the shape a reviewer must see; the
     alternative (rotate the live file at budget/LOG_RETENTION_LAUNCHES so the
     window holds 8 slices) is named in the entry as the veto-open refinement.
     MEASURED BY: the mutant run's file sizes, scaled -- the mutant reproduces
     the same shape at 4 MiB.

8.  THE DISCRIMINATION BETWEEN RULE (c) AND RULE (a) -- the storm, measured
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
A second mutant, identical except that it rotates THE CHANNEL BEING WRITTEN
(the section's option (a)), runs on a farm whose log directory is SEEDED with
one 6 MiB shader.1.log -- a channel the show never writes -- and the same 4 MiB
budget.  Predictions:
8.1  Rule (c): the shader channel is the largest, so it rotates; the seeded file
     walks up the indices one rotation per logged line and is deleted when it
     reaches index 7.  I predict at most 8 in-session rotations, ALL naming
     `shader`, within the first few dozen logged lines, after which the run
     behaves exactly like 4.x above.  MISS if any rotation names another channel
     before the shader file is gone.
8.2  Rule (a): the seeded 6 MiB is never touched because nothing writes to the
     shader channel, so the total stays above the budget forever and EVERY
     logged line rotates whatever channel it was written to.  I predict
     >= 500 in-session rotations in a 10 s run (vs <= 8 under rule (c)), none of
     them naming `shader`, the seeded file still present at 6 MiB at exit, and
     spacecrafter.log growing by ~450 B per rotation -- growth without bound,
     which is the defect.  MEASURED BY: the D12 line count and the seeded file's
     survival.  A ratio below 10x between the two arms is a MISS and the rule
     choice goes back to the section's (a).
