================================================================ (i) THE FRAME
QUESTION (mandate 1(i)): in which frame does a child's
positionAtTimevInVSOP87Coordinates output land on the OLD path -- the parent's
EQUATORIAL frame (z = spin axis), or an ecliptic-parallel one?

ANSWER: an ECLIPTIC-PARALLEL (root / VSOP87-oriented) frame centred on the
parent.  Never the parent's equatorial frame, never its surface frame.  Three
independent citations, each sufficient on its own:

  1. Body::get_heliocentric_ecliptic_pos SUMS ecliptic_pos up the parent chain
     with NO rotation at any hop [observed: body.cpp:619-626].  A sum of
     vectors from differently-oriented frames would be meaningless; the code
     that consumes it (the observer's own heliocentric position,
     AnchorPointBody::update [anchor_point_body.cpp:73-76]) treats the result
     as a VSOP87 vector.
  2. Body::computeDraw composes the model matrix as
     T(p_ecl) . T(b_ecl) . R_b . R_p  [observed: body.cpp:992-1011].  Its
     TRANSLATION is p_ecl + b_ecl -- the child's offset enters unrotated.
     (The rotation half, R_b . R_p, is in the opposite nesting order from
     getRotEquatorialToVsop87's R_p . R_b [body.cpp:552-559]; that is an
     old-path inconsistency of the ORIENTATION half only, it does not touch
     the position, and it is not this task's -- S11.52(b).)
  3. The providers themselves do the conversion.  OrbitCreatorEliptic reads
     the PARENT's obliquity / ascending node / J2000 longitude and hands them
     to EllipticalOrbit [observed: orbit_creator_cor.cpp:43-96], whose
     constructor bakes them into a 3x3 `rotate_to_vsop87` applied to every
     output [observed: orbit.cpp:394-436].  So "deliver root-aligned output"
     is the PROVIDER's contract on this path -- which is exactly the contract
     LocationOrbit does not honour.
  Cross-check on the new path: ModularBody.hpp:655-664 states the same as a
  measured contract ("Every ephemeris output ... is a vector in the ROOT
  (ecliptic VSOP87) orientation ... hops between bodies are PURE TRANSLATIONS,
  plus the surface fold for boundToSurface bodies"), harness 2026-07-11.

CONSEQUENCE, and it is a THIRD defect S5.21 does not name: the shipped
LocationOrbit writes spheToRect(lon + spin, lat) * r straight into
ecliptic_pos [observed: orbit.cpp:1099-1105], so its `lat` is measured from
the ECLIPTIC plane and its `lon` runs about the ECLIPTIC pole.  The authored
point is not on the parent's surface at the authored planetographic
coordinates for ANY parent whose equator is tilted -- i.e. for every parent
in the shipped file.  The missing factor is the parent's
getRotEquatorialToVsop87().

OLD'S OWN AUTHORITY for "where is planetographic (lon, lat, alt) on body P" is
the observer's, and it is one line:
    AnchorPointBody::getRotLocalToEquatorial(jd, lat, lon, alt)
      = Z((P->getSiderealTime(jd) + lon) * pi/180) . Y((90 - lat) * pi/180)
      [observed: anchor_point_body.cpp:63-64]
    composed with P->getRotEquatorialToVsop87()
      [observed: anchor_point_body.cpp:69-71]
Applied to (0,0,r) the Y/Z pair is exactly spheToRect(sid+lon, lat) * r.

WHERE THE NEW PATH PUTS THE SAME CHILD.  ModularBody::recursiveUpdate hands
GROUNDED children `mat` -- the parent's ACCUMULATED EQUATORIAL frame -- and

    not grounded -> eclipticPos lives in the ROOT-ALIGNED frame (== old's)

=============================================================== (ii) THE SPIN
Each path's ONE rotation authority, read at EVALUATION:

WHAT THE SHIPPED CLASS DOES INSTEAD [observed: orbit.cpp:1090-1092, 1099-1105]:
it freezes `parentOffset = P->getSiderealTime(0)` and
`JDToRotation = 2*pi/parentPeriod` at CONSTRUCTION and extrapolates linearly
from JD 0 -- 2.45 million days before J2000.  Both are carried through `float`
locals [observed: protosystem.cpp:557-558] on the old path.  Three costs:
  (a) the parent's period and phase are FROZEN: a runtime
      `body name <parent> rot_periode ...` no longer reaches the child (I2 --
      the child holds a degraded duplicate of the parent's spin state);
  (b) on Earth the parent's law is the APPARENT sidereal time, which no linear
      model follows (quadratic term + the equation of the equinoxes);
  (c) the float truncation of a ~1 day period is multiplied by 2.45e6 days.
(a)(b)(c) are what the probe measures; the magnitudes are P4/P5.

LIFETIME (I5), if the spin is to be read at evaluation:
    OLD:  Body holds `std::shared_ptr<Body> parent` [observed: body.hpp:637] and
          its children as RAW pointers, `std::list<Body *> satellites`
          [observed: body.hpp:638] -- so a strong reference from a child's Orbit
          to its parent closes no cycle, and the child's Orbit is owned by the
          child, which already holds that strong reference.  A
          `std::shared_ptr<Body>` (or a raw pointer guarded by it) is

    NEW:  ModularBodyPtr -- the project's destruction-notified form, which also
          REDIRECTS when a body is replaced by name; SurfacePointOrbit already
          holds its parent that way [observed: SurfacePointOrbitLoader.hpp:109-114].
    ORDERING HAZARD for whoever lands the frame half on the OLD path:
    SolarSystemDisplay::computePositions runs compute_position for EVERY body
    BEFORE computeTransMatrices runs compute_trans_matrix for any
    [observed: solarsystem_display.cpp:342-379].  getSiderealTime(jd) is a pure
    function of jd and is safe to call there; the parent's
    rot_local_to_parent / axis_rotation MEMBERS are one frame stale at that
    point and must NOT be read -- the rotation has to be recomputed from the
    parent's elements, as compute_trans_matrix itself does.
