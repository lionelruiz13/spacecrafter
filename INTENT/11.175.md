# INTENT §11.175

175. **PRECONDITION GATE AT TASK INITIATION — owner-stated protocol rule: broken precondition = task abort; landed in the executor definition (constraint), §0.7 (event-bound gate step), §0b.3 (dispatcher states preconditions in checkable form) (2026-08-30, Claude Fable 5; protocol change, RECORD-ONLY on the code side).**

    - **(a) The mandate, verbatim [vixy 2026-08-30]:** *"validate the precondition of a task before initiating a task - precondition broken = task abort, without that a breakage leak into a task silently, may or may not resurface later, and compromise work based on broken premise."* Placement beyond the named file left open by the owner (*"and maybe it should also land elsewhere"*) — resolved by me [derived], recorded in (c).

    - **(b) The forcing argument, decompressed [derived].** A task spec is a cached conclusion: its premises were true at mint time, not necessarily at warm-up — the dispatch gap is a premise-staleness window. A breakage absorbed at initiation propagates silently: every downstream artifact inherits the broken premise, the work can look green throughout, and whether the breakage ever resurfaces is not under the executor's control — §11.174 is the motivating instance class at full scale (two rounds ran on a silently-broken display precondition; the fault surfaced only because a close report happened to show the delta, *"it might have never been reported"* [vixy]; ten deliveries' photometric absolutes went suspect retroactively). Abort is UNCONDITIONAL by the decision-ownership constraint: adapting to a broken premise is an improvised decision the executor does not own (§11.174(h): *"different path doesn't certify same preconditions"* [vixy]; the mitigation-masking lesson — F43's competent repair hid the fault from the one person who could fix it properly). The asymmetry closes it: under sequential supervised dispatch an abort costs one round-trip; a premise-compromised delivery costs a correction sweep of unknown extent.
