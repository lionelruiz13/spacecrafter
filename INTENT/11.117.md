117. **B39 — `hidden` MEANS AS-IF-NONEXISTENT IN THE RENDERED UNIVERSE, AND NOTHING ELSE (2026-07-30, Opus 5 executor dispatched by Claude Fable 5, task F11; code `5b488e84` instrument + `a62ad5d7` behaviour; harness `83b4c0a` RED baseline, `6521c40` green, + this entry's commit).** D23's mandate [vixy 2026-07-26, verbatim at §11.113(b)] is implemented STRUCTURALLY rather than as a set of guards: `hide()` takes the parked subtree OUT of the per-system sorted list, which is the list every render sweep walks, so ONE edit makes ten separate contributions leave the frame with nothing left to forget. §5.31 CLOSES on its own discriminator (hidden orbit line **779 px → 0**, against a shown-body control that stays 779 px). B19's tick RETIRES into the §11.76 D8 barrier and the retirement is a **measured fact**, not an inference (a parked body: **2074 → 0** orbit evaluations over ~2070 rendered frames). The trail's missed span is RECONSTRUCTED from the orbit at unhide and shown to lie ON the orbit to **0.0315 %** of a prediction made from the same run's data. Field data pristine on every launch (`03fbee59` / `545a51ef`); old path unchanged by construction.

    - **(a) THE ROOT, and why it is not a gate.** §5.31 names the defect as a missing visibility test in the orbit LINE sweep, but the same row names the actual cause: *"`hide()` never deregisters from `sortedSystemBodies` (`removeBody` is destruction-only)"*. That list is FLAT and per-system, and it is what `drawSystemBodies`, `drawOrbits` (both sweeps), `drawTrails`, `drawTails`, `computeShadows` (self-shadow nomination, caster scan, receiver scan), `findBodyAt` and the sort/cleanup all iterate [observed: `ModularSystem.cpp`]. Guarding each sweep would have been one guard per sweep forever, plus the ancestor case each would have to re-derive. `hide()` now calls `propagateRenderHidden`, which walks the parked subtree, and `show()` reverses it — so **orbit line, trail DRAW and trail RECORDING, comet tail, the body draw (with its hints/labels, axis, planet grid, halo and its depth trace, i.e. occlusion), shadow casting, shadow receiving, self-shadow nomination and click-picking** all stop for a hidden body as a side effect of nesting, which is exactly the shape D23's last clause asks for (I6: rework the structure; the alternative was ten instances of one class).
      **The literal reading of §5.31 was NOT taken, and the reason is a semantics boundary, not taste** [derived]: the TRACE sibling's test is `if (!body) continue;` = `operator bool` = *on screen AND not hidden*. Adding that to the LINE sweep would also delete the orbit line of every body outside the view cone — a user-visible change D23 does not ask for and which §11.39's own comment argues against (*"the cached frame is correct for EVERY body, visible or not … the halo-only planets whose orbits were misplaced pre-fix"*). The gate implemented is the HIDDEN half alone. Recorded so the divergence from the row's wording is visible rather than silent.

    - **(b) DECLARED vs EFFECTIVE, the clause that makes the nesting case work.** `relation` stays the DECLARED value — D23: *"mustn't change the exposed hidden attribute/flag"* — and a new member `renderHidden` carries the EFFECTIVE one (own declared relation **OR** any ancestor's). `propagateRenderHidden` is the single writer of BOTH the flag and the list membership, in the same walk, so the two cannot drift apart (I2/I3), and it ORs each node's own relation on the way down, which is why a descendant hidden independently stays out when an ancestor is shown again — **the old path's §5.44 defect, absent here by construction rather than by a guard**. `ModularBody::operator bool` now asks `renderHidden` instead of `relation` (§11.111 could not see the nesting case) and `ModularBody::draw` asks `operator bool` instead of re-spelling its expression — the copy predated §11.111's hidden clause, so the two answers to *"does this body belong to the drawn surface"* could disagree, and did, for a hidden camera reference.
      **MEASURED, both halves**: the selection pointer on **Io under a hidden Jupiter** drew **137 px** before and **0 px** after, against a parent-shown control of 138/136 px; Io's own `relation` stayed **4** throughout, and Earth's stayed **4** with the **Sun** parked (the deepest case: the observer's own reference is a child of the parked node).

    - **(c) THE TICK IS RETIRED, and what replaced the frame it used to carry.** `updateHiddenBodies` — a translation-only recursive walk of every parked subtree, every frame — becomes `publishParkedFrame`: one `Mat4f` copy, and only for nodes that own a parked child. It publishes the ONE thing a parked body cannot recompute for itself: its parent's flat position frame. That is load-bearing and was not obvious — `dispatchUpdate`'s up-chain loop never writes `matLocalToBodyPos` (it writes `mat` only), and **the shipped hidden bodies hang off `Sun`, which is precisely an up-chain ancestor for an Earth observer**, so reading the parent's cache instead would have read a stale frame on exactly the corpus that matters. The six publish sites are the six the retired walk used, and the GROUNDED variant's surface frame is derived from the published flat frame by the identity every one of those sites already relied on.
      Everything else recomputes **at the use**, through `ModularBody::useNow`: the §11.76(b) barrier, with **+4 extra iterations** [vixy-specified] because `EllipticalOrbit::eccentricAnomaly` and `IterativeEll`/`IterativeHyp` advance exactly ONE Newton step per call from the previous call's seed [observed: `orbit.cpp:515-560`, `iterative_orbits.hpp:93-101`] — a body that stopped being evaluated left that seed at its hide date, so one step from it is not the position at `jd`. The constant lives once (`RESUME_EXTRA_ITERATIONS`, consumed by the barrier and by the trail reconstruction). The barrier is idempotent per frame (`evaluatedJD`, stamped at the two — and only two — places a body's position becomes current), so **a use every frame costs what the tick cost and no use costs nothing**, which is the whole point.
      **USE CHANNELS ENUMERATED AND INSTALLED** (§11.76(b) requires the enumeration to be part of the optimization): unhide · becoming the camera reference (`switchToBody`/`warpToBody`, BEFORE `calculateSwitchCompensation` reads the cached hops of `dst` and of every node up to the common parent) · the system's LIGHT SOURCE · the SELECTION (refreshed once per frame in `updateSystem` rather than at each of a dozen `ModularObject` reader sites, I2) · the dual_dump.

    - **(d) "NO TICK" IS BOUNDED BY THE REFERENCE CHAIN, and that is the mandate, not an exception** [measured]. A parked subtree leaves the *sweeps* unconditionally, and leaves the *update walk* whenever the camera's reference chain does not pass through it — which is where the saving is. It does NOT leave the update walk when the chain does pass through it, because the walk enters at the reference and climbs: with the **Sun parked**, `Sun`, `Earth` and `Moon` were still evaluated **+1499 / +1499 / +1499** times over 6 s, while `Jupiter` and `Io` parked off-chain measured **+0 / +0** over ~2070 frames. That is §11.113(b)(vi)'s own rule expressed in the mechanism (*"being the reference is itself a use, so 'shouldn't tick' cannot mean 'holds no state'"*), and it is why `S10.sts` needs no barrier at all: its hidden home_planet is on the chain and therefore current by construction.

    - **(e) "AS IF THEY NEVER WERE HIDDEN" — the module edge, and the one residual D23 named.** `BodyModule::resumeAfterHidden` delivers the unhide edge to each module, which answers for its own state (I4). `OrbitModule`/`HintModule` snap their DISPLAY fader to the target it would have settled at: those faders advance in WALL time and froze with the sweep, so without this an orbit line or a label switched OFF while the body was hidden would fade out AFTER the body came back (HintModule's header already recorded the freeze as a known consequence of tick-at-draw; for the HIDDEN case D23 makes *settled* the required answer). `TrailModule` reconstructs the samples it did not take, from the body's own `Orbit` at their own dates, on the module's declared cadence — or, where the past is not a function of time, restarts fresh and **LOGS** it (§2.0 D12, §2(f) shape). That log branch is the ONE named residual of §11.113(b)(iv); it is **unreachable on today's corpus** [observed: a parented body cannot exist without an orbit — `ModularBody`'s ctor evaluates `orbit->…` unconditionally] and is implemented as the type-level completion rather than left to a future caller.
      **THE RECONSTRUCTION IS CHECKED FOR GEOMETRY, NOT PRESENCE.** `TrailModule::dumpState` gained `tailJD` + `pathLength`, so the recorded history's shape is observable: after a 60-day hidden span the trail carried **64 samples == the predicted 4 + 60 at `deltaTrail` = 1 d**, and the polyline's mean speed (**1.395520e-02 AU/d over 105.001 d**) matched the h/r prediction built from the same run's measured heliocentric distances (**1.4548831 / 1.5121006 / 1.5866192 AU** → predicted ratio 0.975499 vs measured 0.975806) to **0.0315 %**, band 0.5 %. Every input measured in-run; no recalled datum (§11.51(d)). Second-order terms derived rather than absorbed: the radial component cancels to ~0.01 % in the ratio, and the pre-hide leg's 15-day chords under-measure their arc by 0.079 % (from the ini-declared `orbit_visualization_period = 686.98`).
      **A DIVERGENCE FROM THE CONTROL, stated because it is real**: a never-hidden body driven by 15-day date JUMPS records only 4 samples over the same 60 days — `accumulate()` appends at most one per call, and the source says the loss is by design (*"detail lost on big jumps"*). The reconstruction uses the module's DECLARED cadence, i.e. what the trail would have had under continuous time; the two agree wherever time flows continuously and diverge only in the jump regime. The gate therefore asserts the PREDICTION, not equality with the control.

    - **(f) THE INSTRUMENT THAT MAKES THE RETIREMENT A FACT.** `evalCount` (dumped per body) counts entries into the two orbit-evaluation sites — the `accumulateCount` class of §11.56. Committed FIRST, alone, so the RED baseline and the green run read the same instrument. Side benefit exploited throughout: on a body that is always evaluated it IS a frame counter, inside the process, identical in both binaries.
