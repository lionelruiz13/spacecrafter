Four of the five shipped command forms are now DUAL, measured against old's own behaviour through the live command channel; the fifth (`align_with`) is recorded as a derivation gap with the measurement that closes the question, and `transition_to body`'s HEADING TAIL is recorded as not-ported with the measurement and the rule that forbids it. Old `AnchorManager` untouched — comparison baseline by construction (§11.52(b)). Config/ssystem md5 pristine (`03fbee59` / `545a51ef`), every measurement on a fresh launch under the temp-HOME farm with the `/proc/<pid>/comm` concurrent-instance assert.

    - **(a) THE STRUCTURAL ANSWER, and it is F7's continued: A TRAVEL MOVES THE PLACE.** The old path animates a travel by writing a heliocentric position onto the current anchor every frame (`anchor_manager.cpp:310` + `getTravelPosition:406`). §11.111(a) already answered what a place is on the new path — *an anchor is a place, and a place is a body* — and a body's position IS its orbit (`transformParentToBodyPos` evaluates it and nothing else writes `eclipticPos`). So a travel is a **re-declared motion law**: `ModularBody::setOrbit` installs a `TravelOrbit` on the anchor body for the duration. There is still exactly one position authority (I2), it is still evaluated by the tree, and — like old's — it is a pure function of the DATE, so a dropped frame or a time jump lands where the date says rather than where the frames happened to accumulate. The alternative (a per-frame position write beside the orbit) would have been a second authority for "where is this place at date jd", which is the same mistake §11.111(a) refused for the anchor kinds themselves.
      The refusal predicate falls out of the same structure and stays meaningful: old refuses to travel when `typeid(*currentAnchor) == typeid(AnchorPointBody)`, i.e. **you can only travel if you are standing on a place you can move**. The new-path spelling is `CameraAnchors::currentPlace` — the current anchor, if it OWNS its body and the camera references it — asked of the anchor layer rather than of the body's type, because "is this a place" is that layer's own fact (I4). ATTACHED anchors and bare body references are bodies, and a body is not travelled.
      **The speed curve is old's, transcribed, quirks included**: logistic on `x = 25f − 5` with `u = 2`, `s = 1`, clamped — which starts `1/(1+e^7) = 9.110512e-04` of the distance ALONG (a pop at t0) and ends `1/(1+e^18) = 1.523e-08` SHORT (it never exactly arrives). Both are reproduced rather than cleaned up: this is a port and old is the baseline. Both are also what makes the parity check discriminating — a merely "smooth S-curve" implementation passes an eyeball and fails on these two numbers.

    - **(b) WHERE THE OBSERVER IS became expressible, and the composition was DISCRIMINATED rather than derived on paper.** The transitions ask a question the camera's spherical triple cannot answer: *where is the eye, in a frame some other body also lives in*. Four methods answer it — `Camera::getReferenceRelativePosition` / `positionRelativeTo` / `getRootPosition` / `placeAt` — one inverse pair plus its re-expression, all derived from `viewMat()`, **the matrix the renderer consumes**: for an affine map `[R|t]` taking a frame to the eye, the eye's position in that frame is `−Rᵀ·t`. Deriving it from the drawn matrix instead of from the pose members is what makes it convention-proof (right in freeMode and anchored, bound and unbound, with or without the B17 view offset — a left-multiplied rotation cancels) and is what keeps it from drifting from what is on screen (I2).
      The paper derivation of the surrounding frames was WRONG twice, and the instrument said so. First run: `rootPos` came out as **minus** the observer's heliocentric position, because `calculateSwitchCompensation`'s direction is not what its doc comment reads like. Rather than re-argue, a temporary dump block emitted `viewMat`, `computeSurfaceToBody`, `accumulatedBodyToBodyPos`, the compensation and the cached root position, and **twelve candidate compositions were scored offline against the old path's own observer position on the same frame**: `accᵀ·(−Rᵀt) + refRoot` won at **2.63e-08 AU**, and the three families of rival (undoing the surface fold, transposing the tilt, flipping the sign) missed by **1.07e-05 / 3.21e-05 / 8.53e-05 AU**. 2.63e-08 AU is the float32 floor of a 1 AU subtraction (1 AU · 2⁻²⁴ = 6e-08), so the winner is not merely best — it is exact to the arithmetic available. The surface fold turned out to be a factor INSIDE `viewMat`'s map, not a frame outside it, which is exactly what `viewMat`'s own declaration says and what the paper derivation had ignored.
      **The comparison is legal because the root frame IS heliocentric on this data — measured, not assumed**: `Sun`, `SolarSystem` and `MilkyWay` all dump `ecl` **[0,0,0]** `[measured: artifacts/f33/f33_smoke.json P3]`. The fixed-point anchors of §11.111(d) had assumed it; it is now a recorded observable.
      `getRootPosition` accumulates in **double**: the terms are ~1 AU and the answers are often ~1e-05 AU, so float32 would lose 6e-08 AU (9 km) to cancellation at every transition. `Camera::dumpTrace` gains `rootPos` (17 digits locally, for the same reason) — the field that makes a travel comparable between two paths whose pose triples are expressed against different references.

    - **(c) `transition_to point` — the place takes the observer's position, so nobody moves.** Old captures `observer->getHeliocentricPosition(jd)`, then sets the altitude to 0, so the observer ends up exactly ON the new point and does not move (`anchor_manager.cpp:528-547`). Same here: the eye's root position is read BEFORE the point moves (if the camera is already standing on that very point, moving it first would move the observer with it), the point is placed there through the same `installTravel` with a zero duration, the camera switches to it through F7's own `switchTo` (so the kind semantics and the D12 universe-frame log are the anchor layer's single authority), and `placeAt({0,0,0})` zeroes the distance. A zero-length placement keeps longitude and latitude, which parametrize nothing at a centre — as old leaves them alone there too.
      **MEASURED** `[artifacts/f33/f33_result.json M1]`: old's observer moves **6.684e-13 AU**, new's **1.800e-08 AU**, the two paths still agree on where it is to **4.510e-08 AU**; the camera references `temp_point`, `distance` is **0**, the surface bind is off, the registry reports kind `point`; and the absolute look direction is held to **6.584e-07 rad** against the measured per-switch `recoverParams` Euler floor of 4.8e-07 (§11.111(h)).
      **The command's documented `name` is DROPPED by the interface** — the anchor is always `temp_point` (`app_command_interface.cpp:4243`), on both paths, because the dual seam hands both the same hard-coded string. Asked for `Space`, got `temp_point`, measured. Recorded as **§5.82**, not respelled: honouring it would change what 19 shipped lines DO.

    - **(d) `move_to point` — the travel, per step, against a law committed before the run.** The prediction is in `f33_transitions.py::predict_travel`, committed at harness `c9dbd73` BEFORE the measuring run (F29/F32 discipline). **TIME IS STEPPED, NOT RUN**: both travels are pure functions of the date, so the clock is paused and the date is set explicitly at each of 8 fractions of the duration. Without that a 3 s travel over ~1 AU moves 5.6e-03 AU per frame at the shipped cadence, and any inter-path frame skew would swamp a comparison whose subject is 1e-7 AU; stepping removes the frame-rate coupling entirely.
      **MEASURED over f = 0, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 0.9, 1.0, 1.25 of a 600 s travel across 2.588 AU** `[artifacts/f33/f33_result.json M2]`: worst `|old − predicted|` **6.682e-13 AU** (old computes in double), worst `|new − predicted|` **4.996e-08 AU**, worst `|old − new|` **5.120e-08 AU** — all at the float32 floor of the place's `Vec3f eclipticPos`, which is the mechanism the tolerance was set from before the run. The two quirks land: the START POP measured **9.110498e-04** of the distance against the predicted **9.110512e-04**, and the arrival is short by **2.404e-08** relative against the predicted 1.523e-08 plus a float32 endpoint floor of 4.6e-08 relative. The travel flag retires on the step past the arrival date, on both paths.

    - **(e) `move_to body` — aim where the body WILL BE, stop `altitude` km short.** Old computes the target from `Body::getPositionAtDate(jd + travelTime)`, normalizes, and shortens the trip by `radius·5` (no altitude given) or `radius + altitude/AU` (`anchor_manager.cpp:459-486`; `AU` is in km, so the command's `altitude` is kilometres, and the radius is the SCALED one — `body.cpp:486`). Mirrored term for term with `ModularBody::getPositionAtDate`, which is `Body::getPositionAtDate`'s shape on this tree (the sum of the parent-relative orbit of every hop up to the root), including its documented limit: the sum is of raw orbit outputs, so a GROUNDED hop is not folded — exact for the ORBITING chains every shipped travel target has, and identical to old's limit rather than quietly different from it.
      **MEASURED, 5 stepped fractions of a 600 s travel to Mars with `altitude 50000`** `[artifacts/f33/f33_result.json M3]`: worst `|old − new|` **3.943e-08 AU**; at arrival the observer stands **3.5694191e-04 AU** from Mars' centre (old: **3.5694521e-04**, delta **3.30e-09**) against the predicted `scaledDatumRadius + 50000/AU` = 2.27075416e-05 + 3.34229e-04 = **3.569366e-04 AU** — the residual (+5.3e-09 new, +8.6e-09 old) is the never-quite-arrives quirk of (a) at this travel length (1.523e-08 × 0.97 AU = 1.48e-08 AU), i.e. a residual PREDICTED from the attributed cause, not fitted.
      Two old-path degeneracies were found here and recorded, not inherited: the body form has no `time == 0` branch, so `duration 0` divides by zero there (**§5.83**; the new path treats a zero-duration travel as its endpoint, because a NaN would poison the tree's cached frame state and the D8 barrier rejects NaN dates by design), and `getPositionAtDate` leaves the iterative solvers' Newton seed at the future date (**§5.84**; the new path re-converges at the frame's own date at the call site, the §11.117 `useNow` remedy).

    - **(f) `transition_to body` — the observer stays where it is, and the pose says so exactly.** Old spends two bisections finding the longitude/latitude that reproduce the place it is already at, and sets the altitude to the distance it measured (`anchor_manager.cpp:549-672`); its latitude search stops at `interval > 0.01`, so it lands with a residual. The camera's pose IS that triple, so the same place is expressed directly: the eye's offset is read in the TARGET's frame before the switch, F7's `switchTo` performs the reference change, and `placeAt(offset, holdView)` puts it back.
      **MEASURED** `[artifacts/f33/f33_result.json M4]`: the observer moves **0.009 km** on the new path against **0.803 km** on old (old's own bisection residual — the new path is exact where old approximates, which is a difference in old's favour that the parity bar explicitly allows: `new ≤ old`); the camera references Mars; the pose `distance` **3.683490e-04 AU** is the distance to the body's centre to 4.1e-09; and the absolute look direction is held to **7.036e-07 rad**.
      **THAT LAST NUMBER WAS 1.5707e-02 rad UNTIL THE CAMPAIGN CAUGHT IT**, and the fix is code `204d402e`. The mechanism is `Camera::update`'s EQUATORIAL-mount latitude interception, which re-derives the view whenever `latitude != foldLat` to keep the ZENITH-frame direction across an observer latitude MOVE (old-mount parity). A held placement is the other case: the observer stands still and only its parametrization changes — a new reference expresses the same place at a different latitude — so the interception turned the sky by exactly that difference (measured 1.5707e-02 rad of absolute look direction against a 1.6346e-02 rad latitude change, the interception to the digit), which A38 (*the reference switch holds the WHOLE orientation*) forbids. `placeAt(holdView)` now writes `foldLat` with `latitude`, **before** `recoverParams` solves. **The ORDER is the fix and it was measured**: written after, `recoverParams` solves against the old fold and the new fold then applies the very rotation the write was meant to prevent — bit-identical 1.5707e-02, a null result that would have read as "wrong hypothesis" had it not been re-derived. The shipped config is `viewing_mode = equator`, so this fired in the field.

    - **(g) THE HEADING TAIL IS NOT MIRRORED, and the reason is a rule, not a shrug.** Old's `transitionToBody` ends with `navigator->setHeading(-angle)` where `angle = body->getAxisAngle()`, then `changeHeading(0, 5000)` (`anchor_manager.cpp:661-669`). **MEASURED live** `[artifacts/f33/f33_result.json M4]`: the tail fires — `flagChangeHeading` true, `startHeading` **49.139°**, `endHeading` **0**, caught mid-ramp at **41.29°** / **41.49°** on two runs. So old's committed END STATE at every `transition_to body` is heading 0, reached over 5 s from minus the body's axis angle.
      Two terms, and both fail, which is why nothing was ported:
      **T2, `getAxisAngle`**: it is not a property of the body — it is the SCREEN-PROJECTED angle of its rotation axis, computed inside `Body::computeDraw` through the active PROJECTOR from the PREVIOUS frame's matrices (`body.cpp:1052, 1083`; `axis.cpp:76-101`). The new path computes no such quantity anywhere: `AxisModule` has no angle. Its value is also not a function of the state at command time (it is 0 for a body that was not drawn last frame), so there is no state-to-state law to reproduce.
      **T1, `changeHeading(0, 5 s)`**: the spelling ports exactly (`Camera::setHeading(0, 5)`) and the heading conventions ARE the same by construction (one config key, `heading`, feeds both `Navigator::setHeading` and `Camera::setHeading` at `SSystemFactory::loadCamera`). But forcing heading to 0 at a reference switch is a ROLL DECISION AT A REFERENCE SWITCH, which is D28's open question — inherited at this clause, exactly as §11.111(c) inherits it for the kind-(2) switch — and it is already answered the other way for this path by **A38**: the switch holds the whole orientation. Old ends with `local_vision` uncompensated and heading 0; the new path ends with the absolute orientation held and the heading param rewritten by `recoverParams` (measured: −0.383 rad → −5.426 rad while the physical direction moved 7.0e-07 rad — §11.111's "the param-heading moves while the orientation is held", now measured on this member too). Porting T1 alone would therefore not reproduce old's image; it would impose a NEW roll on a held view. Recorded, not invented.

    - **(h) `align_with` — the derivation gap, and the measurement that makes it a verdict rather than a shrug.** Per F25's term-table method (§11.133), old's math first. `Navigator::alignUpVectorTo(rot, duration)` (`navigator.cpp:428-480`) builds `A = mat_local_to_eye · mat_earth_equ_to_local · mat_equ_to_vsop87ᵀ` — which maps **vsop87 → eye**, the map its own comment asks for (*"we bring this vector in the camera frame"*) — and then applies `Aᵀ`, the INVERSE. It also converts the target axis **vsop87 → j2000** first (`mat_vsop87_to_j2000`), a frame no other factor in the expression speaks. Term by term against the new path: `mat_local_to_eye` ↔ `renderViewRotation()` (modulo old's `+0.0001` rad heading fudge), `mat_earth_equ_to_local` and `mat_equ_to_vsop87` ↔ `placementRotation()` and `accumulatedBodyToBodyPos` (their local-frame convention difference cancels because they are adjacent), the target's `rotEquatorialToVsop87` ↔ its `accumulatedBodyToBodyPos`, and `changeHeading` ↔ `setHeading` (endpoints exact, ramp curve perceptual per §11.52(b)). **The two terms with no camera-side expression are the transpose and the j2000 hop.** They are numerically transcribable, so the honest question is whether they encode a requirement — and that is measurable.
      **MEASURED through the live command channel, two black-box discriminators that need no frame algebra** `[artifacts/f33/f33_result.json M5]`: `align_with body Mars` is **NOT IDEMPOTENT** — a second call moves the heading another **25.158°** (0 → −122.614 → −97.456) — and it is **START-DEPENDENT** — from heading 40° it lands **29.635°** away from where it lands from heading 0° (40 → −92.979 → −113.701). An operation that makes the axis vertical is both. **So old does not meet the requirement its own shape appears to encode** (and the author's inline note says so: *"I can't get the axis of the planet at the moment"*). There is nothing to port: porting would mean transcribing a frame error to reproduce a number that means nothing. Recorded as **§5.85**, with what is owed before it is judged (what the command is FOR — only its author or a show that wants it can say). 0 shipped scripts use it. The new path's heading is measured UNTOUCHED by the command (0, 0, 0 across the sequence) — the member is absent, not half-present.

    - **(i) THE REFUSALS ARE PART OF THE PORT, and they are measured per path.** The dual seam returns `oldOk || newOk`, so a member that accepted what old refuses would also change the script's answer AND its clock (the command interface's `wait` rides that verdict). The new path's messages carry §2(f)'s three parts (what fired, what state it left, the fix action).
