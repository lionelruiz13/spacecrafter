## (a) The row, and the condition it attached to its own fix

§5.34: both `Object::operator=` overloads overwrote `rep` after retaining the new one
and never released the old, so **every reassignment of an `Object` leaked one
reference** `[observed: tools/object.cpp:109-127 @ bd3f7117]`. The row refused the fix
in the selection wave for a stated reason (§11.106(h)): *"releasing correctly changes
object lifetime on the old path — a star wrapper that today survives by leaking would
be destroyed — so it needs its own enumeration of who holds raw `ObjectBase*` and its
own verification."* The enumeration therefore came first, and the fix was conditional
on it.

## (b) THE ENUMERATION — every holder of a raw `ObjectBase*`, and every raw wrapper
pointer that escapes an `Object`

Method: exhaustive over `src/` minus `EntityCore`, on the three shapes a raw rep can
take — a declared `ObjectBase *`, an escape from `Object`, and a counted holder that
could hand one out.

**1. Declared `ObjectBase*` — the complete list is FOUR lines, and three of them are
`Object`'s own** `[observed: grep `ObjectBase *\*` over src/ minus EntityCore]`:
`Object::rep` (`object.hpp:117`), the two `Object` members that take one as a parameter
(`object.hpp:44,47`, plus the `operator==(ObjectBase*)` comparison at `:113`), and the
two free functions `intrusivePtrAddRef` / `intrusivePtrRelease`
(`object_base.cpp:35-43`), whose parameter is transient by construction.
**No other translation unit in the tree declares, stores or caches an `ObjectBase*`.**
That single fact is what makes the fix safe, and it is why the enumeration had to be
done rather than assumed.

**2. The four refcounted rep classes, and who mints them.** `ObjectBase::retain/release`
are virtual **no-ops** (`object_base.hpp:56-57`); four subclasses override with
delete-at-zero — `StarWrapperBase` (`hip_star_wrapper.hpp:67-73`), `ModularObject`
(`ModularObject.hpp:24-30`), `TullyWrapper` (`TullyWrapper.hpp:35-41`), `Star3DWrapper`
(`Star3DWrapper.hpp:35-41`). Every one of the four is **heap-minted per query and owned
by nothing else**: `Star1/2/3::createStelObject` (`hip_star_wrapper.cpp:246-259`, reached
from `HipStarMgr::searchHP/search*/searchAround`), `new ModularObject(...)`
(`ssystem_factory.cpp:785, 808` — the only two sites), `new TullyWrapper` (`tully.cpp:486`),
`new Star3DWrapper` (`starNavigator.cpp:572`). So for those four an `Object` assignment
**can be the last release**, i.e. can destroy the wrapper it drops — which is exactly what
the row warned about. `Body`, `Nebula`, `Constellation` keep the no-op pair and are owned
by their managers (`ProtoSystem`, `NebulaMgr`, `ConstellationMgr`), which is why the
defect stayed invisible for years.

**3. Long-lived holders — all counted, none raw.** Four `Object` members exist in the
tree: `Core::selected_object` and `Core::old_selected_object` (`core.hpp:695-696`),
`SolarSystemSelected::selected` (`solarsystem_selected.hpp:110`),
`SSystemFactory::selected_object` (`ssystem_factory.hpp:1064`). Every other long-lived
holder is an `ObjectBaseP` (the intrusive pointer, correct copy-and-swap
`intrusive_ptr.hpp:44-52`): `Constellation::asterism[]` (`constellation.cpp:70,81`,
released in the dtor `:45`), and the `searchAround`/`search*` result vectors.
`SSystemFactory::selected_object` is the one holder that is **never cleared** — it is
written only for `OBJECT_STAR` (`core.cpp:2318` → `ssystem_factory.hpp:164`) and read as
values (`getSelectedStarRA/DE` `:561,566`, `getRGB` `ssystem_factory.cpp:385`,
`ProtoSystem::load` `:625`), so post-fix it keeps the LAST selected star alive until the
factory dies. That is a holder, not a hole, and it is why the pre-fix star count at
shutdown could have been N−1 (see (d)).

**4. Raw pointers that ESCAPE an `Object` — six call sites, all short-lived.**
`Object::as<T>()` (`object.hpp:107-110`) is the only escape hatch;
`operator==(ObjectBase*)` compares without handing anything out. The six sites:
`solarsystem_selected.cpp:51, 86, 99, 120, 169` (all `as<Body>()`, a type whose
retain/release are no-ops and whose lifetime belongs to `ProtoSystem`) and
`ssystem_factory.cpp:771` (`as<ModularObject>()`, used in the next statement while the
caller's `Object` is alive). **None of the six outlives its `Object`, and none of the six
is stored.**

**5. Selection sets keep NAMES, not reps.** `HipStarMgr::setSelected(Object)` takes the
Object **by value** and stores only `std::map<std::string,bool>` + `std::vector<int>`
(`hip_star_mgr.cpp:1109-1121`, `hip_star_mgr.hpp:535-536`);
`NebulaMgr::setSelected(Object)` stores a `std::set<std::string>`
(`nebula_mgr.cpp:356-360`); `ConstellationMgr::selected` is
`std::vector<Constellation*>`, manager-owned (`constellation_mgr.hpp:218`).
`ConstellationMgr::setSelected(const Object&)` and `Constellation::isStarIn(const Object&)`
compare by **english name** (`constellation.cpp:277`), holding nothing.

**6. Frame-consumers read through the `Object`, never around it.** `old_selected_object`
is consumed every frame by `skyDisplayMgr->draw(..., selected_object.getEarthEquPos(nav),
old_selected_object.getEarthEquPos(nav))` (`solarSystemModule.cpp:210`,
`stellarSystemModule.cpp:205`); the pointer draw is `selected_object.drawPointer(...)`
(four executor sites); `Navigator::updateVisionVector(int, const Object&)` reads
`selected.getEarthEquPos` (`navigator.cpp:53-62`). All value reads through the handle.

**VERDICT — the stop boundary is NOT hit.** No holder depends on the wrapper outliving
its last retaining `Object`: there is no raw holder to depend on it, the six escapes are
all intra-expression, and the two consumers that DO span selection changes
(`old_selected_object`, `SSystemFactory::selected_object`) are themselves counted
`Object`s that retain what they read. The fix was therefore in scope.

**Anti-recurrence (F29's writer-enumeration precedent).** This enumeration is now the
class's own header documentation (`tools/object.hpp`, above `class Object` and at
`as<T>()`): the ownership contract, the four counted rep classes, the escape rule, and
the "managers keep names" fact. A future holder is added against the list rather than in
ignorance of it — the list is the structural half of the fix.

## (c) The fix

`tools/object.cpp`, both overloads: **retain the new, then release the old.**

```
const Object &Object::operator=(const Object &o)
{
	ObjectBase * const previous = rep;
	rep = o.rep;
	rep->retain();
	previous->release();
	return *this;
}
```

and the same shape for `operator=(ObjectBase* const r)`, with
`rep = r ? r : &ObjectUninitialized::instance;`.

Three judgements, each with its argument:

1. **The `if (this != &o)` guard is GONE, replaced by the order.** The guard covered
   only literal self-assignment; retain-before-release covers that AND the wider case of
   two different `Object`s holding one rep, where release-first could destroy the very
   thing being assigned. For literal self-assignment the new code is a net zero
   (retain then release on the same rep), so nothing observable changes for the case the
   guard did cover.
2. **The null branch now retains too.** Pre-fix, `obj = (ObjectBase*)nullptr` assigned
   `&ObjectUninitialized::instance` **without** retaining it, while `~Object` releases
   unconditionally — an asymmetry that is inert only because that instance inherits the
   no-op pair. Making it uniform costs nothing and removes the second instance of the
   same class of bug from the same function.
3. **Scope stayed `Object`'s own special members, and the other three were checked, not
   assumed.** The copy ctor retains (`:103-107`), the value/pointer ctors retain
   (`:91-101`), the dtor releases (`:86-89`) — all already correct. There are no move
   operations: a user-declared copy ctor + dtor suppress the implicit ones, so there is
   no move hole to fix and adding moves would be a performance change, not this fix.

## (g) What the leak was costing, beyond the bytes

Two consequences worth having on the record now that they are gone:

1. **`ModularBodyPtr::ref` grew without bound** and is walked linearly by
   `~ModularBodyPtr` (`while (ref[++i] != this);`) and entirely by `redirect`, which runs
   **on every body destruction** (`ModularBody.cpp:251`) and on every replacement
   (`:111`). Each composed selection added one permanent entry, so a long show made every
   subsequent body destruction slower, for ever.
2. **`ModularBody::pointerCount` is a `uint8_t`** (`ModularBody.hpp:2054`) used as the
   boolean gate on that redirect (`if (pointerCount) ModularBodyPtr::redirect(this, parent)`).
   Pre-fix each selection of a composed body incremented it and nothing ever decremented
   it, so **256 selections of one body wrapped the counter to 0** and the body's
   destruction would then skip the redirect, leaving every leaked `ModularBodyPtr` holding
   a dangling `ptr` — a use-after-free at the next `--(ptr->pointerCount)`. `[derived
   from the sources cited; NOT reproduced — 256 selections of one body were not driven]`.
   Post-fix the count is balanced by construction, so the wrap is unreachable by
   selection.
