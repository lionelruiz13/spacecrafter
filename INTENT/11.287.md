    - **(a) WHY THE ENGINE FIRST** [derived from three owner-stated generators, each recorded today]: (1) *"Reduce the amount of things to keep track of"* (§11.284(h)) — an engine is the one place where the cross-cutting concerns (lifetime, ordering, dispatch, the data format) are handled ONCE, so that no other piece tracks them; writing everything separately makes every piece track its own, N things instead of one; USING a foreign engine hands over the tracking but at the price of its contract surface, which must be acquired and then HELD — a large foreign contract is itself a thing to track. (2) The cost rule of §11.285(g) — *"rebuilding the tools is often easier than learning existing ones"* — decides between the two: build the coordination layer unless the host's contract is small or its mode set large and needed; Phocan is the proof case (§11.280(c)2): inside Unreal he still built the coordination layer (the VM and the database) and consumed Unreal through a bounded surface — the foreign engine demoted to a HOST. (3) *"What survived the rewrite has always been the structural findings, the primitives"* (§11.284(g)) — the engine is WHERE the primitives live: each rebuild re-derives them in the new language (entitylib2 Py -> entitylib C -> entitylib2 C -> EntityLib -> EntityCore, §11.286), so the engine is the persistence medium of his learned structure across projects, and D10 (optimize the potential) is what it buys: a coordination layer's potential is the product of what can be built on it, separate pieces have only their own. So the engine-first reflex is not a preference for engines; it is the three generators meeting at one object. The delta from usual practice is real [knowledge]: most either adopt an engine whole (its model held in the head) or write the application directly (each piece coordinating itself); the coordination layer built first and small is the rare shape.
