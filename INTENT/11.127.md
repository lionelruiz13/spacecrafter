### The fix, at the root rather than at the instance

`clearDepth` does three things: it sets the depth **mapping**, it clears the attachment,
and it takes the per-body command-buffer boundary. The depth-less band needs the first
and must not pay for the other two — a full slice entry per body is exactly the cost the
band exists to avoid, and D3 says this band holds thousands of bodies. So the mapping was
separated from the slice:

- `Renderer::enterDepthlessSlice(zCenter, boundingRadius)` — the mapping alone. Depth
  test and write are off, so the range only has to BRACKET the body; it uses the same
  `[d-r, d+r]` form `clearDepth`'s own out-of-coverage fallback uses.
- called from **both** spellings of the regime ladder — `ModularBody::draw` (inline) and
  `ModularBody::drawLoaded` (the load-checking copy) — after the far components, so the
  band keeps the same hint-behind-disc order the depth band has.
- `clearDepth` stops assuming nobody writes the range between two same-bucket bodies: it
  re-reads its bucket's range on **every** call instead of only at bucket entry. Same
  values (the range is the bucket's, and the bucket is the authority), two extra loads,
  one fewer assumption — a mid-band body can sort between two members of one bucket, and
  now its override cannot outlive it. Without this the fix would have introduced the
  defect it removes, one regime up.

## (b) §5.54 — px-intent, DERIVED, and the census was wider than the row

**The census** [observed, whole `src` grep]: `0.2` had **six** spellings
(`ModularBody.hpp`, `ModularBody.cpp`, `LayeredMesh` ×2, `BasicMesh`, `PhotosphereModule`),
`0.0015` **three** (`ModularBody.hpp`, `ModularSystem.cpp` ×2 — the latter two being the
draw-entry gate re-spelled, the same class B39/§11.117 closed for `operator bool`),
`0.008` two. Half were `double` literals compared against a `float`. The row recorded two
spellings; there were eleven.

**PIXELS, and the argument is four independent lines that agree:**

1. G4 verbatim — *"compute only what the observer can distinguish"*. What an observer
   distinguishes is pixels. `b12-design.md`'s *"smallest added structure ≥ 1 px"* is the
   same criterion written out, and it is what set B12's own regime boundary.
2. Every other member of this family already converts [observed]: the sibling in the same
   constant block, `SYSTEM_VISIBILITY_SUBSYSTEM_SIZE`, is consumed as
   `px = (θ/halfFov)·2·viewportRadius; if (px >= SIZE)` (`ModularSystem.cpp:748-752`);
   the orbit-bucket gate is `5.f / viewportRadius` under a comment that says *"absolute"*
   (`Renderer.cpp:50-52`); `AtmExtModule.cpp:116` (10 px), `RingModule.cpp:150`, and
   `drawHalo` (`ModularBody.hpp:1572-4`) — whose comment records that the unconverted
   form **was a bug**. The family's working members are px; its broken one was not.
3. The old path, which is the parity baseline by construction, is absolute px throughout:
   `screen_sz < 180` (`body_bigbody.cpp:441`, `body_smallbody.cpp:113`), `needOrbitDepth`
   at 10 px.
4. D5 spans 1k–8k. Under fraction-intent the 16 px full-visibility gate is 65 px at 8k:
   the same body drawn as a surface on a 2k dome is halo-only on an 8k one. More
   resolution, less drawn — G4 backwards.

Nothing supports fraction-intent. This is a derivation, not a preference.

**The structure.** The px constants are the authority; the `screenSize` forms the draw
path compares in are derived by `ModularBody::setViewportRadius`, which is now the
viewport's **only** writer — so there is no state in which the gates disagree with the
viewport (I3), and `viewportRadius` stops being public data. Eleven literal sites become
five named gates read through accessors; `ModularObject.cpp:200`, which re-read the
scissor to recompute the same half-width, joins them.

**The depth-bucket gate had to come along.** `Renderer::clearDepth`'s stated precondition
is that the notable threshold sits below the full-visibility one. As fractions
(0.004 vs 0.008) that order **inverts above ~4k** — 16.384/8192 = 0.002 < 0.004 — so a
body would draw with depth while holding no bucket, and the out-of-coverage fallback
would fire every frame. In px (8.192 < 16.384) the precondition holds by construction at
every width. This is a real consequence of px-intent found by checking the change against
the full historical scope rather than the triggering case.

**Two gates where there was one literal at 0.2.** `BODY_CLOSE_RANGE_BOUNDING_SIZE` (the
near/grounded/in ladder) and `BODY_BIG_TEXTURE_BOUNDING_SIZE` (which texture level a mesh
binds) are two questions, and §5.53(b) has an open one about the texture gate alone.
Equal today, separately addressable — merging them would have made §5.53(b) unanswerable
without moving the regime ladder too. Judgment call, flagged.

### VETO POINT (recommend + implement reversibly, never silently decide)

Values are the shipped literals re-expressed in the pixels they mean at the 2048-wide
render they were frozen at: **3.072 / 8.192 / 16.384 / 409.6 / 409.6 px**. At 2048 the
derived gates are **bit-identical** to the old float literals — verified arithmetically
(dividing by 2048 is a power-of-two scaling, so `3.072f/2048 == 0.0015f` exactly) and
observed in the app's own reported gates (`early: 0.00150000001`).

**The discrepancy this exposes is left standing, deliberately:** the two constants that
carried NAMES said **2 px** and **16 px**. Full-visibility matches (16/2048 = 0.0078,
shipped as 0.008); early-visibility does **not** — the shipped gate is **3.07 px**, not 2.
Behaviour was preserved (D8 as-if: no silent user-visible change), and which value is
right is a product question about when a body stops being a dot. **Vixy's.** Reversing it
is a one-token edit of one constant. Also for that decision: `RAYMARCH_MIN_SCREEN_SIZE`
(0.025 ≈ 51 px at 2048) is the one screenSize threshold still spelled as a fraction —
single-site, no I2 duplication, and a raymarch *capability* gate rather than a G4 regime
boundary, so it was deliberately not converted; the px argument applies to it by the same
reasoning, and it is named in-source at its declaration.

## (c) §5.53(a) — root-caused, and the row's own premise is what proves it

The row reasons: *a downscale preserves the mean, so the cause is in the reduction/cache
path*. The premise is correct. **The reduction path is faithful, and that is the finding.**

`f18_level.py` measures the disc-mean ratio across the big-texture gate — the ratio, so
that lighting and projection cancel (the two captures differ only in fov, so the
illuminated hemisphere is identical).

| body | preview file? | normal/height maps? | measured ratio reduced/full | predicted |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Jupiter** | no | no | **[0.998, 0.998, 0.998]** | 1.000 — a downscale preserves the mean |
| **Sun** | `sun-preview.jpg` | no | **[1.016, 1.257, 2.352]** | [1.013, 1.257, 2.365] from the two FILES' means |
| **Moon** | `moon-preview.jpg` | yes | **[1.460, 1.473, 1.465]** | [1.286, 1.307, 1.303] from near-side file means |

**Root cause** [observed `tools/s_texture.cpp:157-163` + measured]: when `resolution` is
set, the loader looks for an authored `<name>-preview.<ext>` beside the texture and, if it
exists, **uses it as the reduced level instead of downscaling anything** — the
full-resolution file is then only consulted for its dimensions. That is a capability, not
a bug. What is a bug is that the two shipped previews are photometrically inconsistent
with their partners [measured, the files themselves]:

- `sun-preview.jpg` mean **[254.0, 246.2, 223.5]** vs `sun.jpg` **[250.7, 195.9, 94.5]**
- `moon-preview.jpg` mean **[184.1, 183.3, 181.5]** vs `moon.jpg` **[150.5, 147.0, 146.1]**

The Sun's rendered step reproduces its two files' mean ratio to within **0.5 %**. Exactly
**two** bodies are affected tree-wide — they are the only `tex_map` values with previews —
and the row's Sun measurement was one of them; the **Moon is the other, and nobody had
noticed**. The prediction that discriminates was fixed before the runs and is different in
kind per body: a code-side transform would give ONE signature everywhere, per-file data
divergence gives each file's own. The Sun's is strongly colour-shifted (1.02/1.26/2.35),
the Moon's is neutral (1.46 on all three, within 0.9 %) — different shapes, as predicted.

The Moon's **magnitude** exceeds its colour-map prediction: 1.46 against 1.29 for the
near-side hemisphere (the whole-map mean is the wrong comparator — the far side is
brighter, and the ratio over the visible half is 1.286/1.307/1.303). The excess, ×1.13, is
**achromatic to 1 %**, and the Moon is the one body of the three that also swaps a NORMAL
and a HEIGHT map at that gate: sharper normals raise shading contrast, and Lambert's
clamp at zero makes that asymmetric — a darkening of the full level. Named as the
candidate, not claimed: the discriminating measurement would be a body with a normal map
and no preview, which the corpus does not contain.

**Two scene-selection corrections worth carrying forward**, both caught by the instrument
rather than by luck: at the b12 date the Moon is near new (disc mean 5.3/255, where
quantisation crushes any ratio toward 1) and Mercury is likewise unlit. The lit date was
found **by measurement** — elongation from the app's own dump over a 30-day sweep, full
moon at jd 2461251.5, elongation 175.3° — never from recall.

**NOT decision-free, so not fixed. The fork, named:**

- **(A) Correct the preview assets** — regenerate `sun-preview.jpg` / `moon-preview.jpg`
  as true downscales, or re-author them to match. **Vixy's channel**: data is the product
  and the installed field is frozen (§2.0 D9), so corrections propagate forward through
  `spacecrafter-data` only. This is the recommendation.
- **(B) Make the loader reject an inconsistent preview** — rejected: it would silently
  override authored data, and an authored low level legitimately *may* differ (that is the
  capability's whole point). There is also no cheap consistency test.
- **(C) Log the substitution** (D12: acting defaults are logged) — **BUILT and WITHDRAWN.**
  The log was placed at the loader's preview branch, built, and **never fired**, while the
  preview's pixels were demonstrably on screen; the string is in the binary [verified with
  `strings`], so the branch does not execute in the steady state. A diagnostic that cannot
  be shown to fire is a silent no-op probe, and shipping it would convert observation into
  fiction — the same bar §5.59's fix was withdrawn against. The placement question needs
  the texture-cache path understood first, which is its own task. Reverted; the tree
  carries no §5.53 code change. A second attempt (an unconditional probe at
  `asyncLoad` entry) **aborted the app at startup** — recorded as a warning to the next
  task that this path does not tolerate a naive log.
